# Mac screen recording guides

<p class="lead">Practical guides to recording your Mac's screen, checked against Apple's documentation, with the built-in tools first. Where Avora, a free open source recorder for Mac, does something the built-in tools don't, the guides say so and show how.</p>

## [How to stop a screen recording on Mac](/blog/how-to-stop-screen-recording-on-mac)

Stop a screen recording on Mac with the Stop button in the menu bar or Command-Control-Esc, find the button when it is hidden, and stop Avora from any app.

## [How to screen record on Mac](/blog/how-to-screen-record-on-mac)

How to record your screen on a Mac with the Screenshot toolbar, QuickTime Player or a free app: whole screen, a window or an area, with sound and permissions.

## [Record Mac system audio without BlackHole](/blog/record-mac-system-audio-without-blackhole)

Why people install BlackHole to screen record with audio on Mac, how the Multi-Output setup works, and the ways to record system audio with no virtual driver.

## [How to record screen and audio on Mac](/blog/how-to-record-screen-and-audio-on-mac)

Record your Mac's screen with your voice and the Mac's own sound: the built-in options by macOS version, then a voice-over with camera and captions.

## [Where do screen recordings go on Mac?](/blog/where-do-screen-recordings-go-on-mac)

Mac screen recordings are saved to the desktop as .mov files by default. How to find them, change where they go, and where Avora keeps its recordings.

## [OBS alternatives for Mac, compared](/blog/obs-alternatives-for-mac)

OBS Studio is built for streaming. Other ways to record your screen on Mac, from the free built-in tools to Screen Studio and Avora, compared side by side.

## [Record your screen to a GIF on Mac](/blog/screen-recording-to-gif-mac)

macOS records screen video as .mov, not GIF. How to record your screen straight to a GIF on Mac, convert an existing recording, and keep the file small.

## [Auto zoom in screen recordings: how it works](/blog/auto-zoom-screen-recording)

How a screen recorder with auto zoom decides where and when to zoom: click windows, focus regions, springs, motion blur and a cursor drawn after recording.
