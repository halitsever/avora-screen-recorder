# Add captions to a video on your Mac

<p class="post-meta">By <a href="https://halit.org">Halit Sever</a> · <time datetime="2026-11-28">28 November 2026</time></p>

<p class="lead">Captions make a screen recording usable with the sound off, which is how many people first watch a video in a feed or a chat. There are two kinds: captions drawn into the picture, and a separate caption file that viewers turn on. A Mac can make both from your voice.</p>

## Burned-in or closed captions

| | Burned in (open) | Closed (.srt file) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seen by | Everyone | Viewers who turn captions on |
| Can be turned off | No | Yes |
| Works where | Any player, any site, a chat preview | Players and sites that load caption files, such as YouTube |
| Editing later | Needs a new export | Edit the text file |

For a short clip shared in Slack, a social feed or an email, burned-in captions are the safe choice. For a video on YouTube or a course platform, upload an .srt file. [What is an SRT file](/blog/what-is-an-srt-file) explains the format.

## Make captions with Avora

[Avora](/) is a free, open source screen recorder for Mac (macOS 14 or later) that writes captions from what you said:

1. Open **Settings…** and find **Captions**.
2. Turn on **Burn captions into the video**, **Save captions as an .srt file**, or both.
3. Pick the **Language**, or keep **Same as this Mac**.
4. Record with your microphone on. Captions are made when you export.

The speech is turned into text with Apple's speech recognition, on the Mac whenever the language supports it. Avora uses the microphone track, or the Mac's own sound when no microphone was recorded. macOS asks once for permission to use speech recognition.

## Check and fix the words

Speech recognition mishears names and product terms. Before you export:

1. Choose **Edit Last Recording…** (<kbd>Command</kbd> <kbd>E</kbd>) from the menu bar menu.
2. Click **Captions** in the editor's toolbar (or **Redo Captions** if they exist).
3. Select a caption in the list, fix its text and, if needed, set its start or end with **Start at Playhead** and **End at Playhead**. An empty caption shows nothing.
4. Export. Burned-in captions appear as white text on a dark rounded box, and the .srt file is saved next to the video, timed to the edited version.

## Without Avora

On a Mac without extra software, you can type an .srt file by hand in TextEdit and upload it with the video to a site that supports caption files. QuickTime Player does not create caption files. For a recording you have not made yet, it is quicker to record with captions turned on. [Download Avora](https://github.com/halitsever/avora-screen-recorder/releases/latest).

## FAQ

### What is the difference between captions and subtitles?

Captions are meant for viewers who cannot hear the audio and follow what is said; subtitles usually translate speech for viewers who can hear it. Both can be burned in or delivered as an .srt file.

### Are the captions made in the cloud?

In Avora, the speech is recognised with Apple's speech recognition, on the Mac whenever the language supports it.

### Can I burn captions and save an .srt file at the same time?

Yes. Turn on both options in Avora's Captions settings.
